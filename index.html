Places with a past.

Walk through living history, from red sandstone walls to elegant avenues and gardens made for a slower afternoon.

Old Delhi · UNESCO site

Red Fort

Shah Jahan’s great sandstone fortress, with palaces, pavilions and the Lahori Gate that opens onto Chandni Chowk.

Allow 2 hours · check weekly closing day
Old Delhi · Mosque

Jama Masjid

One of India’s largest mosques. Climb the minaret for a rooftop view over the tangled lanes below.

Go early or near sunset · cover shoulders and knees
Bahapur · Architecture

Lotus Temple

A white, petal-shaped House of Worship set in calm gardens. Quiet, open to all, and wonderfully photogenic.

Silence inside · short queue before noon
Lodhi Road · Garden

Lodhi Garden

Fifteenth-century tombs among old trees, joggers and picnickers. The best free hour in the city.

Golden light at 5 pm · free entry
02 / The streets

Markets that never sleep.

Bargain a little, taste a little, get lost a lot. Each market has its own rhythm and its own reason to visit.

Old Delhi

Chandni Chowk

The city’s oldest and busiest artery. Narrow lanes of silver, fabrics, wedding cards and sweet shops. Take a cycle rickshaw and keep your bag in front.

Old Delhi

Khari Baoli

Asia’s largest wholesale spice market. Sacks of chillies, saffron and dried fruit; the air itself makes you sneeze and smile.

INA

Dilli Haat

An open-air craft bazaar with stalls from across India, plus a food court serving regional dishes. A fair-priced place to shop for gifts.

South Delhi

Sarojini Nagar

Budget fashion and fast bargains. Go with patience, a light bag and a sense of humour.

Lodhi Colony

Khan Market

Bookshops, bakeries and cafés on a leafy lane. Browse a second-hand shelf, then linger over coffee.

Connaught Place

Janpath & CP

Handicrafts, tibetan stalls and colonial-era arcades arranged in perfect white circles. Easy to reach by Metro.

05 / Before you go

Travel notes.

Small things that make the trip smoother, learned the slow way.

Getting around

The Delhi Metro is clean, quick and the best way to cross the city. Use app-based cabs or auto-rickshaws for short hops, and agree on the fare first.

What to wear

Light cotton, comfortable shoes you can slip off, and a scarf or stole for mosques and temples. Carry a hat in summer.

Etiquette

Remove shoes at places of worship, ask before photographing people, and dress modestly at religious sites.

Stay smart

Buy monument tickets from official counters or sites, keep valuables close in crowds, and sip only bottled or filtered water.

WinterNovember – February

Crisp mornings and clear days: the best time to explore. Fog can delay early flights and trains.

SummerApril – June

Very hot. Sightsee early and late, rest through the afternoon, and stay hydrated.

MonsoonJuly – September

Warm, humid and green. Gardens look lovely; carry an umbrella and expect traffic slowdowns.

07 / Questions

Good to know.

How many days do I need?

Three days covers the highlights at an easy pace. Add a fourth for Humayun’s neighbourhood, Hauz Khas or a day trip to Agra if you can.

Is Delhi safe for first-time visitors?

Yes, with normal city care. Keep bags zipped in crowded markets, use licensed taxis or cab apps after dark, and stay aware of your surroundings.

Which is better, Old or New Delhi?

They are different moods. Old Delhi is crowded, loud and full of flavour. New Delhi is spacious and formal, with wide avenues and gardens. See both.

Do the monuments close on certain days?

Some do, so check each site’s official page before you go. Early mornings are cooler and calmer almost everywhere.
